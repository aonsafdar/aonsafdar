01 About

I am a doctoral researcher at University College Dublin and a Cohort 5 fellow of ML-Labs, the Research Ireland Centre for Research Training in Machine Learning. The project, Customising ViT Tokenizers for Improved Performance in Medical Imaging, looks at how an image becomes the sequence a transformer actually reads. Medical images are not natural photographs. The useful signal is often texture, channel structure, and what survives a change of scanner, rather than the layout of objects.

Before UCD I completed an MSc in Computer and Software Engineering at the National University of Sciences and Technology (2020–2022). The doctoral work is supported by Taighde Éireann – Research Ireland under Grant 18/CRT/6183.

02 Research

Medical-imaging vision transformers

The thesis customises tokenization and the axis of global attention. CoMViT replaces rigid patches with a convolutional tokenizer, uses diagonal masking and learnable temperature, and pools the sequence instead of a class token, at about 4.5 million parameters. It was presented at MIRASOL 2025, held with MICCAI. Later models treat the axis itself as the variable. FAD-ViT (ACCV 2026) and ADViT (WACV 2027) make feature-axis attention the primary mixer and leave spatial structure to depthwise operators. CAViT takes a lighter step in the same direction: dynamic attention over channels in place of the static MLP, presented at the T4V workshop at CVPR 2025 and extended at AICS 2025.

Foundation models, transfer, and sensing

HABShield (BRACIS 2026) is a benchmark for harmful-algal-bloom detection from MODIS ocean colour and bathymetry, built so that model scale, pretraining domain, and the channel interface can be separated. A companion IEEE GRSL letter uses that benchmark to test whether any-sensor transfer needs physical wavelength conditioning, or whether a channel-flexible encoding is doing the work.

I also work on integrated sensing and communication at KAUST, on sensing from passive cell-side 5G uplink data, with the same interest in foundation models and generalization. A public paper for that line is not yet available.

Agentic AI for research

With Mohamed Saadeldin I have been studying what a general-purpose language-model agent does when it is given an open vision-architecture question and the tools to pursue it. The IEEE ICA 2026 paper is a longitudinal case study of that trace. A poster at the NeurIPS 2026 AutoMLR workshop looks at the behavioural signature of the same study. The extended preprint is arXiv:2608.01995.
